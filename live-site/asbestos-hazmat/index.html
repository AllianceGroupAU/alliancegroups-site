What we remove.

Most asbestos in Brisbane homes is bonded asbestos cement — the flat and corrugated “fibro” sheeting used through the 1950s to the 1980s. We also handle friable material that needs the tighter controls of a Class A licence.

  • Fibro walls and ceilings — bathrooms, laundries, enclosed verandahs, extensions and wall linings.
  • Asbestos cement roof sheeting — corrugated roofs, gutters, downpipes and roof capping, with the new roof to follow.
  • Eaves and soffit sheeting — often the first thing a painter or electrician disturbs.
  • Wet-area splashbacks and underlay — behind tiles, under showers and beneath old vinyl.
  • Vinyl floor tiles and backing — including layers hidden under newer floor coverings.
  • Meter boards, fences, sheds and outbuildings — the small jobs that still need doing right.
  • Friable asbestos — damaged or crumbling material, lagging and fire-damaged sheeting.

Queensland licenses asbestos removal by the type of material. Alliance holds the capability for both, so one contractor can take the whole job.

How the job runs.

  1. Photos and site details. You send the address, photos and what has been found. We work out whether an inspection or testing is needed first.
  2. Scope and price. We confirm the material, quantity, access, containment, disposal and clearance requirements, then give you the price before work is booked.
  3. Notifications and set-up. Required notifications are lodged, the work area is isolated and signed, and controls are set up for the material involved.
  4. Removal. Material is removed whole where possible, kept wet, wrapped and labelled. Nobody else enters the work area.
  5. Clean-up and clearance. The area is decontaminated. Where required, an independent assessor inspects and issues the clearance certificate.
  6. Waste and records. Waste goes to a licensed facility. Photos, waste documentation and completion records are kept with the job.

After removal.

Removal leaves a hole — a bathroom without walls, a roof without sheeting, a verandah without lining. Most asbestos contractors leave at that point. We do not have to: Alliance can carry the job through to new linings, roofing, carpentry and reinstatement so the room is back in use.

Asbestos questions

Asbestos removal, plainly answered.

Still unsure? Call 0403 126 276.

Do I need a licensed removalist to remove asbestos? +

In Queensland a licence is not required to remove 10 m² or less of non-friable (bonded) asbestos, but the work must still follow Part 8.7 of the Work Health and Safety Regulation 2011. More than 10 m² of non-friable asbestos needs a Class B (or Class A) licence. Any amount of friable asbestos needs a Class A licence. Read the full rules.

What is the difference between friable and non-friable asbestos? +

Non-friable (bonded) asbestos is bound into a material such as cement sheeting, so it does not crumble by hand while it is in good condition. Friable asbestos is loose or can be crumbled, pulverised or reduced to powder by hand pressure, so it releases fibres far more easily and needs a Class A licence to remove.

Do you test for asbestos? +

Appearance alone cannot confirm asbestos. Where identification is needed, a sample is analysed by an accredited laboratory. We will tell you whether testing is worth doing before any removal is quoted.

Can you remove an asbestos roof and put a new one on? +

Yes. We remove asbestos cement roof sheeting under the required controls and can carry out the replacement roof and associated repairs. See roofing and asbestos roof replacement.

What happens to the asbestos waste? +

Asbestos waste is wrapped, labelled and taken to a facility licensed to accept it, and the waste documentation is kept with the job record.

Will I get a clearance certificate? +

Where the work requires it, an independent competent person or licensed asbestos assessor carries out the clearance inspection and issues the clearance certificate before the area is reoccupied.

Can tenants stay in the property? +

Occupants must stay out of the asbestos removal work area. Whether other areas may stay occupied depends on the risk assessment, the separation that can be achieved and the controls required for the job.
